American History: US Citizenship Test Practice Questions

46 practice questions from this part of the 2025 civics test. Answer out loud, then open “Show answer” to check it and read the explanation.

Independent practice. Not affiliated with USCIS or the U.S. government. The official questions and study guide are free on uscis.gov. Read them first; this page does not replace them.

1. The colonists came to America for many reasons. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Freedom
  • Political liberty
  • Religious freedom
  • Economic opportunity
  • Escape persecution

Many Jamestown colonists wanted economic opportunity, such as land to farm. The Pilgrims came in 1620 for religious freedom.

2. Who lived in America before the Europeans arrived?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • American Indians
  • Native Americans

Native Americans, also called American Indians, lived in the Americas for around 20,000 years before the Europeans arrived.

3. What group of people was taken and sold as slaves?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Africans
  • People from Africa

Millions of enslaved Africans were taken to North and South America; the first group was brought to Jamestown in 1619.

4. What war did the Americans fight to win independence from Britain?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • American Revolution
  • The (American) Revolutionary War
  • War for (American) Independence

The Revolutionary War lasted from 1775 to 1783. Great Britain's army surrendered at Yorktown in 1781, and a treaty in 1783 recognized U.S. independence.

5. Name one reason why the Americans declared independence from Britain.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • High taxes
  • Taxation without representation
  • British soldiers stayed in Americans’ houses (boarding, quartering)
  • They did not have self-government
  • Boston Massacre
  • Boston Tea Party (Tea Act)
  • Stamp Act
  • Sugar Act
  • Townshend Acts
  • Intolerable (Coercive) Acts

Colonists believed it was unfair for Britain to tax them without letting them vote for representatives. They wanted to govern themselves.

6. Who wrote the Declaration of Independence?
Show answer

Answer: (Thomas) Jefferson

Thomas Jefferson wrote the Declaration of Independence, which was adopted on July 4, 1776.

7. When was the Declaration of Independence adopted?
Show answer

Answer: July 4, 1776

The Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776, which is why we celebrate Independence Day on July 4.

8. The American Revolution had many important events. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • (Battle of) Bunker Hill
  • Declaration of Independence
  • Washington Crossing the Delaware (Battle of Trenton)
  • (Battle of) Saratoga
  • Valley Forge (Encampment)
  • (Battle of) Yorktown (British surrender at Yorktown)

Important events of the Revolutionary War include the battles of Bunker Hill and Saratoga, and the British surrender at Yorktown in 1781.

9. There were 13 original states. Name five.
Show answer

Accepted answers: Give 5 of these.

  • New Hampshire
  • Massachusetts
  • Rhode Island
  • Connecticut
  • New York
  • New Jersey
  • Pennsylvania
  • Delaware
  • Maryland
  • Virginia
  • North Carolina
  • South Carolina
  • Georgia

The 13 original colonies became the first 13 states when the Declaration of Independence was adopted. All of them are on the East Coast.

10. What founding document was written in 1787?
Show answer

Answer: (U.S.) Constitution

The Constitution was written in 1787 at the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia.

11. The Federalist Papers supported the passage of the U.S. Constitution. Name one of the writers.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • (James) Madison
  • (Alexander) Hamilton
  • (John) Jay
  • Publius

James Madison, Alexander Hamilton and John Jay wrote the Federalist Papers, and signed the essays “Publius.”

12. Why were the Federalist Papers important?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • They helped people understand the (U.S.) Constitution.
  • They supported passing the (U.S.) Constitution.

The 85 Federalist Papers were essays printed in newspapers in 1787 and 1788. They supported passing the Constitution.

13. Benjamin Franklin is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Founded the first free public libraries
  • First Postmaster General of the United States
  • Helped write the Declaration of Independence
  • Inventor
  • U.S. diplomat

Franklin was a Founder who, as a diplomat, negotiated the 1783 peace treaty with Great Britain.

14. George Washington is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • “Father of Our Country”
  • First president of the United States
  • General of the Continental Army
  • President of the Constitutional Convention

Washington led the colonial army in the Revolutionary War and was elected the first President in 1789. He is called the “Father of Our Country.”

15. Thomas Jefferson is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Writer of the Declaration of Independence
  • Third president of the United States
  • Doubled the size of the United States (Louisiana Purchase)
  • First Secretary of State
  • Founded the University of Virginia
  • Writer of the Virginia Statute on Religious Freedom

Jefferson wrote the Declaration of Independence. The Louisiana Purchase, which doubled the size of the country, took place in 1803.

16. James Madison is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • “Father of the Constitution”
  • Fourth president of the United States
  • President during the War of 1812
  • One of the writers of the Federalist Papers

Madison was at the 1787 Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia. He also wrote Federalist Papers essays with Hamilton and Jay.

17. Alexander Hamilton is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • First Secretary of the Treasury
  • One of the writers of the Federalist Papers
  • Helped establish the First Bank of the United States
  • Aide to General George Washington
  • Member of the Continental Congress

Hamilton was at the 1787 Constitutional Convention. With Madison and Jay, he wrote Federalist Papers essays to win support for the Constitution.

18. What territory did the United States buy from France in 1803?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Louisiana Territory
  • Louisiana

In 1803 the United States bought the Louisiana Territory from France: a large area between the Mississippi River and the Rocky Mountains.

19. Name one war fought by the United States in the 1800s.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • War of 1812
  • Mexican-American War
  • Civil War
  • Spanish-American War

For example, the Mexican-American War (1846 to 1848) was fought over Texas and land in the Southwest.

20. Name the U.S. war between the North and the South.
Show answer

Answer: The Civil War

The Civil War was fought between the North and the South from 1861 to 1865, and the North won.

21. The Civil War had many important events. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • (Battle of) Fort Sumter
  • Emancipation Proclamation
  • (Battle of) Vicksburg
  • (Battle of) Gettysburg
  • Sherman’s March
  • (Surrender at) Appomattox
  • (Battle of) Antietam/Sharpsburg
  • Lincoln was assassinated.

The Civil War began in 1861 and ended with the South’s surrender in April 1865. A few days later, President Lincoln was assassinated.

22. Abraham Lincoln is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Freed the slaves (Emancipation Proclamation)
  • Saved (or preserved) the Union
  • Led the United States during the Civil War
  • 16th president of the United States
  • Delivered the Gettysburg Address

Lincoln served as President from 1861 to 1865. He signed the Emancipation Proclamation and was assassinated days after the South surrendered.

23. What did the Emancipation Proclamation do?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Freed the slaves
  • Freed slaves in the Confederacy
  • Freed slaves in the Confederate states
  • Freed slaves in most Southern states

Lincoln signed it in 1863. It announced freedom for people enslaved in most Southern states, but most were not freed until the war ended.

24. What U.S. war ended slavery?
Show answer

Answer: The Civil War

After the Civil War, all enslaved people in the Southern states were freed, and the 13th Amendment made slavery illegal in December 1865.

25. What amendment says all persons born or naturalized in the United States, and subject to the jurisdiction thereof, are U.S. citizens?
Show answer

Answer: 14th Amendment

The 14th Amendment sets the conditions for citizenship by birth in the United States. Naturalization is another way to become a citizen.

26. When did all men get the right to vote?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • After the Civil War
  • During Reconstruction
  • (With the) 15th Amendment
  • 1870

The 15th Amendment, added in 1870, says a male citizen of any race can vote.

27. Name one leader of the women’s rights movement in the 1800s.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Susan B. Anthony
  • Elizabeth Cady Stanton
  • Sojourner Truth
  • Harriet Tubman
  • Lucretia Mott
  • Lucy Stone

Susan B. Anthony fought for civil rights in the 1800s and wanted women to have the right to vote.

28. Name one war fought by the United States in the 1900s.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • World War I
  • World War II
  • Korean War
  • Vietnam War
  • (Persian) Gulf War

The U.S. fought in World War I from 1917 to 1918 and in World War II from 1941 to 1945.

29. Why did the United States enter World War I?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Because Germany attacked U.S. (civilian) ships
  • To support the Allied Powers (England, France, Italy, and Russia)
  • To oppose the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria)

The United States joined World War I in 1917, on the side of the Allied Powers against the Central Powers.

30. When did all women get the right to vote?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • 1920
  • After World War I
  • (With the) 19th Amendment

The 19th Amendment, added in 1920, gave women the right to vote.

31. What was the Great Depression?
Show answer

Answer: Longest economic recession in modern history

The Great Depression was the worst time for the U.S. economy in American history. Banks and businesses closed, and many people lost jobs and homes.

32. When did the Great Depression start?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • The Great Crash (1929)
  • Stock market crash of 1929

The Great Depression started with the stock market crash of 1929. Franklin Roosevelt became President in 1933.

33. Who was president during the Great Depression and World War II?
Show answer

Answer: (Franklin) Roosevelt

Franklin Roosevelt, President from 1933 to 1945, led the country through both the Great Depression and World War II.

34. Why did the United States enter World War II?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • (Bombing of) Pearl Harbor
  • Japanese attacked Pearl Harbor
  • To support the Allied Powers (England, France, and Russia)
  • To oppose the Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, and Japan)

Japan attacked the U.S. naval base at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii, on December 7, 1941. The United States entered World War II the next day.

35. Dwight Eisenhower is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • General during World War II
  • President at the end of (during) the Korean War
  • 34th president of the United States
  • Signed the Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956 (Created the Interstate System)

Dwight Eisenhower was a general in World War II and the 34th President, serving from 1953 to 1961.

36. Who was the United States’ main rival during the Cold War?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Soviet Union
  • USSR
  • Russia

Russia was the largest part of the Soviet Union (USSR). The U.S. and the USSR distrusted each other until the Cold War ended in 1991.

37. During the Cold War, what was one main concern of the United States?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Communism
  • Nuclear war

Communism is a type of government that plans the economy, usually under one political party. The U.S. wanted to stop it from spreading.

38. Why did the United States enter the Korean War?
Show answer

Answer: To stop the spread of communism

In 1950 communist North Korea invaded South Korea. The U.S. fought to stop communism from spreading, its main Cold War concern.

39. Why did the United States enter the Vietnam War?
Show answer

Answer: To stop the spread of communism

Like the Korean War, the Vietnam War was part of the Cold War effort to stop other countries from becoming communist.

40. What did the civil rights movement do?
Show answer

Answer: Fought to end racial discrimination

The movement began soon after World War II. It led to the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

41. Martin Luther King, Jr. is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Fought for civil rights
  • Worked for equality for all Americans
  • Worked to ensure that people would “not be judged by the color of their skin, but by the content of their character”

King organized peaceful protests against racial discrimination. We honor him with a national holiday on the third Monday in January.

42. Why did the United States enter the Persian Gulf War?
Show answer

Answer: To force the Iraqi military from Kuwait

The Persian Gulf War is one of the wars the U.S. fought in the 1900s. It freed Kuwait from the Iraqi military.

43. What major event happened on September 11, 2001 in the United States?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Terrorists attacked the United States
  • Terrorists took over two planes and crashed them into the World Trade Center in New York City
  • Terrorists took over a plane and crashed into the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia
  • Terrorists took over a plane originally aimed at Washington, D.C., and crashed in a field in Pennsylvania

Terrorists hijacked four airplanes: two hit the World Trade Center, one hit the Pentagon, and one crashed in Pennsylvania. Almost 3,000 people died.

44. Name one U.S. military conflict after the September 11, 2001 attacks.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • (Global) War on Terror
  • War in Afghanistan
  • War in Iraq

After the September 11, 2001 attacks, the United States fought in Afghanistan and Iraq as part of the War on Terror.

45. Name one American Indian tribe in the United States.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Apache
  • Blackfeet
  • Cayuga
  • Cherokee
  • Cheyenne
  • Chippewa
  • Choctaw
  • Creek
  • Crow
  • Hopi
  • Huron
  • Inupiat
  • Lakota
  • Mohawk
  • Mohegan
  • Navajo
  • Oneida
  • Onondaga
  • Pueblo
  • Seminole
  • Seneca
  • Shawnee
  • Sioux
  • Teton
  • Tuscarora

There are over 500 American Indian tribes in the United States, including the Cherokee, Navajo and Sioux.

46. Name one example of an American innovation.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Light bulb
  • Automobile (cars, internal combustion engine)
  • Skyscrapers
  • Airplane
  • Assembly line
  • Landing on the moon
  • Integrated circuit (IC)

American innovations include the light bulb and the airplane. In 1969 the U.S. became the first country to land a person on the moon.
