Free US Citizenship Practice Test

Practice the 2025 naturalization civics test with 120 questions, answers, and explanations. Nothing to sign up for and nothing to pay.

Independent practice. Not affiliated with USCIS or the U.S. government. The official questions and study guide are free on uscis.gov. Read them first; this page does not replace them.

How the real test works

  • This is the 2025 test, for Form N-400 filed on or after October 20, 2025. If you filed earlier, your interview may use a different test
  • The officer asks up to 20 of the 128 civics questions aloud, and you answer aloud
  • You need 12 correct answers to pass; the officer stops after 12 right or 9 wrong answers
  • The interview also tests English reading and writing
  • If you are 65 or older and have been a permanent resident for 20 years or more, you may study just the 20 questions marked with an asterisk in the official list. The officer asks 10 of them, and 6 right answers pass

Practice test

Start with a quick 5-question quiz, or take the full test: 20 random questions from the bank. You need 12 correct to pass, like the real test. There is no timer, as in the real interview.

Sample questions

Try these ten out loud first. Open “Show answer” to check each one.

1. What is the form of government of the United States?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Republic
  • Constitution-based federal republic
  • Representative democracy

The United States is a republic: the people elect representatives to govern for them, under the Constitution.

2. Name two important ideas from the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution.
Show answer

Accepted answers: Give 2 of these.

  • Equality
  • Liberty
  • Social contract
  • Natural rights
  • Limited government
  • Self-government

Both documents say that people have rights, such as life and liberty, and that the people govern themselves.

3. How many U.S. senators are there?
Show answer

Answer: One hundred (100)

Each of the 50 states has two U.S. senators, so there are 100 senators in the Senate.

4. What is the highest court in the United States?
Show answer

Answer: Supreme Court

The Supreme Court is the highest court in the United States. No other court can change its decisions.

5. Who wrote the Declaration of Independence?
Show answer

Answer: (Thomas) Jefferson

Thomas Jefferson wrote the Declaration of Independence, which was adopted on July 4, 1776.

6. Name the U.S. war between the North and the South.
Show answer

Answer: The Civil War

The Civil War was fought between the North and the South from 1861 to 1865, and the North won.

7. When did all women get the right to vote?
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • 1920
  • After World War I
  • (With the) 19th Amendment

The 19th Amendment, added in 1920, gave women the right to vote.

8. Martin Luther King, Jr. is famous for many things. Name one.
Show answer

Accepted answers:

  • Fought for civil rights
  • Worked for equality for all Americans
  • Worked to ensure that people would “not be judged by the color of their skin, but by the content of their character”

King organized peaceful protests against racial discrimination. We honor him with a national holiday on the third Monday in January.

9. What is the name of the national anthem?
Show answer

Answer: The Star-Spangled Banner

The national anthem is “The Star-Spangled Banner,” which Francis Scott Key wrote after seeing the flag over Fort McHenry in the War of 1812.

10. Name three national U.S. holidays.
Show answer

Accepted answers: Give 3 of these.

  • New Year’s Day
  • Martin Luther King, Jr. Day
  • Presidents Day (Washington’s Birthday)
  • Memorial Day
  • Juneteenth
  • Independence Day
  • Labor Day
  • Columbus Day
  • Veterans Day
  • Thanksgiving Day
  • Christmas Day

National holidays are also called federal holidays because Congress passed them and the President signed them into law. There are 11 of them.
